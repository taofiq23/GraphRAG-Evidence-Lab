# GraphRAG Evidence Lab

[![DOI](https://img.shields.io/badge/DOI-10.5281%2Fzenodo.22848733-blue)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22848733)

Knowledge-graph retrieval, vector retrieval, and agentic AI evaluation, compared head-to-head on the same question set. A small knowledge graph (people, companies, products, cities) backs three retrieval strategies - **vector RAG** (embedding similarity, the common baseline), **fixed 1-hop GraphRAG**, and **agentic GraphRAG** (the agent decides for itself, via its own LLM self-assessment, how many hops of graph traversal it needs) - each feeding a small local LLM that generates the final answer.

Because the knowledge graph and its QA pairs are generated together, every question carries a known gold answer *and* the exact gold supporting facts, which is what makes the retrieval quality actually measurable rather than eyeballed.

## The core question this benchmarks

Does graph-based retrieval actually help more than plain vector similarity on multi-hop questions - and if it does, does that translate into better final answers, or does the bottleneck just move somewhere else? Both turned out to be true, in that order, which is the interesting part of the result below.

## Benchmark results

Generated by `python -m src.graphrag_lab.benchmark` on 73 QA pairs generated directly from the graph's structure (35 one-hop, 27 two-hop, 11 three-hop). Full output in [benchmark_results/RESULTS.md](benchmark_results/RESULTS.md) and [benchmark_results/results.json](benchmark_results/results.json).

**Answer accuracy:**

| Hops | N | Vector RAG | Fixed 1-hop GraphRAG | Agentic GraphRAG | Agentic avg. hops used |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 35 | 100% | 100% | 100% | 1.4 |
| 2 | 27 | 44% | 0% | **74%** | 2.9 |
| 3 | 11 | 9% | 0% | 9% | 2.8 |

**Retrieval recall** (did the evidence actually contain the gold supporting facts?):

| Hops | Vector RAG | Fixed 1-hop GraphRAG | Agentic GraphRAG |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| 2 | 70% | 50% | **100%** |
| 3 | 36% | 33% | **94%** |

### What this actually shows

- **1-hop**: every system gets it right - the single relevant fact is easy for embedding similarity to find, so there's nothing for graph traversal to add yet.
- **2-hop is where GraphRAG's value proposition shows up cleanly**: fixed 1-hop GraphRAG is structurally incapable of 2-hop questions (0% - it literally cannot see past one edge), vector RAG partially manages by luck (44%, when one of the two needed facts happens to be lexically close to the question), and agentic GraphRAG's adaptive hop expansion reaches 100% retrieval recall and 74% answer accuracy. This is the expected, textbook result.
- **3-hop is the more interesting and more honest finding**: agentic GraphRAG's retrieval recall stays high (94% - the graph traversal keeps finding the right facts, using an average of 2.8 hops), but answer accuracy collapses to 9%, statistically even with vector RAG. **This is not a retrieval failure - it's a reasoning failure.** Handing a 77M-parameter `flan-t5-small` all three correct facts in its context is not enough for it to reliably chain three hops of reasoning into the right final answer. Retrieval quality and reasoning quality are separate bottlenecks here, and fixing one (via graph traversal) doesn't automatically fix the other - a larger/more capable answer-generation LLM would likely be needed to actually realize the benefit of the near-perfect 3-hop retrieval this system already achieves. Swapping `LLM_MODEL_NAME` in `src/graphrag_lab/config.py` to a larger instruction-tuned model is the natural next experiment.
- **The precision trade-off**: agentic GraphRAG's retrieval *precision* is noticeably lower (17-28%) than the other methods, since hop-based neighborhood expansion pulls in real but irrelevant nearby facts (a company's other products, an unrelated employee) alongside the ones actually needed. Higher recall here comes at a real evidence-noise cost.

## Architecture

```
question --> entity linking --> seed entities --> k-hop graph traversal --> evidence
                                                          ^
                                    agent decides: "is this evidence enough?" (LLM)
                                                          |
                                             no -> expand hops, ask again
                                                          |
                                                         yes
                                                          v
                                          evidence --> LLM --> final answer
```

## What it does

- **Synthetic knowledge graph**: hand-authored people/company/product/city facts with `networkx` (`src/graphrag_lab/knowledge_graph.py`).
- **Programmatic QA generation**: 1/2/3-hop question templates instantiated directly from real graph paths, so every example has a verifiable gold answer and gold supporting triples (`src/graphrag_lab/qa_generator.py`).
- **Vector RAG baseline**: `sentence-transformers` embedding similarity over every fact in the graph (`src/graphrag_lab/vector_retriever.py`).
- **Graph retrieval**: exact-match entity linking + k-hop neighborhood traversal (`src/graphrag_lab/graph_retriever.py`).
- **Agentic loop**: the agent retrieves at hop 1, asks its own LLM whether the evidence is sufficient, and expands to hop 2, then 3, only if it says no - a real adaptive-retrieval agent, not a hardcoded hop count (`src/graphrag_lab/agent.py`).
- **Evaluation**: answer correctness (lenient containment match) and retrieval precision/recall against gold facts, broken down by hop count (`src/graphrag_lab/evaluation.py`).
- **Gradio demo**: ask a question, compare vector RAG vs. agentic GraphRAG side by side, with a rendered graph of the retrieved evidence (`app.py`).


## Limitations

- The knowledge graph is **small and synthetic by design** - it exists to make retrieval quality measurable against known gold facts, not to demonstrate real-world knowledge-graph scale or coverage.
- Answer correctness uses a **lenient substring match** (gold answer text appears in the generated answer), standard for evaluating small generative LLMs but looser than exact-match.
- The agent's stopping decision ("is this evidence enough?") is itself an LLM self-assessment with no ground truth backing it - its reliability is one of the things this benchmark surfaces, not an assumption it relies on.
- Entity linking is exact-substring matching against a closed, known vocabulary, which is legitimate for this controlled synthetic domain but is not open-domain entity linking (a much harder, separate NLP problem).
- `google/flan-t5-small` (77M parameters) is used for answer generation, chosen for CPU speed across ~200+ generations in the benchmark. As the 3-hop result shows, it is very likely the accuracy bottleneck at higher hop counts, not the retrieval - swapping in a larger model via `config.LLM_MODEL_NAME` is the natural next step.
